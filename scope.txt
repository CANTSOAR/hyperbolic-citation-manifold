### **Phase 1: Data Acquisition and Graph Construction**

You need to convert raw arXiv metadata into a strict directed acyclic graph (DAG) where edges represent citations.

2. **The Parser (Rust):** * Write a fast parser to extract the `paper_id` and `references` fields.

* Construct an adjacency matrix.


3. **Graph Pruning:** * Remove isolated nodes (papers with zero citations in or out).
* Identify the "roots" (papers that are cited heavily but cite nothing else in the dataset). These are your absolute origin points.

### **Phase 2: The Hyperbolic Architecture**

This is where you define the geometry so that the "gaps" form naturally. You will build a **Hyperbolic Graph Convolutional Network (HGCN)**.

1. **The Manifold:** Initialize a Poincaré Ball manifold using `geoopt`.
* The distance metric between two vectors $x$ and $y$ in this space isn't Euclidean. You will use the Poincaré distance:

$$d_\mathbb{D}(x, y) = \text{arcosh}\left(1 + 2\frac{||x - y||^2}{(1 - ||x||^2)(1 - ||y||^2)}\right)$$




2. **The Encoder:** * Pass the node features (e.g., a TF-IDF or lightweight Transformer embedding of the paper's abstract) through a standard linear layer.
* Use the **Exponential Map** to project those Euclidean features onto the hyperbolic manifold.


3. **The Message Passing (The Springs):**
* Implement hyperbolic graph convolutions. Instead of simple averaging, the model calculates the **Fréchet Mean** of a paper's neighbors. If Paper A cites Paper B and C, Paper A is pulled toward their center of mass in the curved space.

### **Phase 3: The Physics of the "Gaps" (Loss Functions)**

To guarantee that empty spaces naturally form for missing ideas, you must balance attractive and repulsive forces without over-regularizing.

1. **Contrastive Graph Loss (The Push/Pull):**
* **Positive pairs:** If Paper A cites Paper B, minimize their hyperbolic distance.
* **Negative pairs (The Rubber Band):** Randomly sample nodes that do *not* share an edge, and maximize their distance.


2. **Entailment Cone Loss (The Gravity):**
* Enforce the hierarchical structure. If Paper A (new) cites Paper B (old foundation), penalize the model if the norm of B is greater than A ($||B|| > ||A||$). This forces older, highly cited papers inward, and new frontier papers outward.


3. **The Gap Generator (Avoiding Uniformity):** * *Crucial Step:* Do **not** apply a strict global uniformity loss. If you force the points to distribute perfectly evenly across the entire sphere, you will artificially erase the gaps. By only pushing explicitly unrelated clusters away from each other, the exponential volume of the hyperbolic space will naturally tear open vast, empty "saddle points" between isolated disciplines.

### **Phase 4: Training and Infrastructure**

Deploy the training pipeline directly on Deepstack to handle the heavy matrix operations of the Fréchet mean calculations.

1. **Mini-Batching on Graphs:** You cannot load a 2-million-node graph into VRAM. Use **NeighborSampler** to sample localized sub-graphs (a paper and its 2-hop citation network) for each forward pass.
2. **Riemannian Optimization:** Standard Adam won't work in curved space. You must use `geoopt.optim.RiemannianAdam` to correctly update the weights along the curvature of the ball.

How do you prove the model successfully left spaces for undiscovered ideas? You test its predictive intuition.
